What is Hangul (한글)?

Hangul (한글) is the Korean writing system. It was created in 1443 by King Sejong (세종대왕) and officially announced in 1446.

At that time, many ordinary people found it difficult to learn to read and write because they had to use Chinese characters. King Sejong (세종대왕) wanted everyone—regardless of social status—to have the opportunity to become literate.

To achieve this goal, he and his scholars created Hangul (한글), a writing system that was logical, easy to learn, and beautifully designed. Unlike most writing systems, which evolved gradually over hundreds or thousands of years, Hangul (한글) was intentionally created with a clear purpose and design.

Today, Hangul (한글) is recognized as one of the world's most unique writing systems. It is scientifically designed, completely original, and successfully used by an entire nation as its official everyday language.

How Hangul (한글) Was Designed

One of the most remarkable features of Hangul (한글) is that many of its letters were designed to show how sounds are made by the mouth, tongue, teeth, and throat.

ㄱ Back of tongue
ㄴ Tongue touches top
ㅁ Mouth shape
ㅅ Teeth shape
ㅇ Throat shape

Hangul (한글) vowels were also designed using simple ideas from nature:

ㆍ Sky / Heaven
ㅡ Earth
ㅣ Person

아 · 야 · 어 · 여 · 오 · 요 · 우 · 유 · 으 · 이

ㄱ + ㅏ = 가    |    ㅇ + ㅏ = 아    |    엄 + 마 = 엄마
